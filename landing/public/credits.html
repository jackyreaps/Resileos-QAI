Site art, named honestly.

Every portrait on this site is an AI-generated composition. The November 2026 series expands the earlier named figures: sharper volume, bronze and marble, gold accents, and restrained crystal traces. It is not one chrome coating, and it is not a flat museum cast. Floating glyphs and watermarks were taken off. Where a public-domain museum photograph was the identity reference, that photograph is credited below. The portrait on the page is not the photograph.

Metis, Momus, Hephaestus, and the Titanos figurehead had no single public-domain sculpture in this set. Aphrodite, Psyche, Eat, Clotho, Lachesis, Atropos, Harmonia, Calliope, Ligeia, and Echo are also invented for the site. None of those is credited to a museum object. A thorn-crowned bust is not used, and is not labeled as Zeus or any Greek Kin. Short light sweeps on the home hero and the Titanos still are site craft, not pictures the engine generated. Public-domain sources

The underlying works and photographs remain in the public domain or CC0 as noted. Reference files, when kept in the repo, live under assets/gods/cc0/ and are excluded from the public deploy.

Zeus — Marble head of Zeus Ammon

The Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York. Object 257864. Roman, ca. 120–160 CE. Marble. CC0 1.0. Object page. Used for the home hero and Zeus routing.

Apollo — Marble head of Apollo

The Metropolitan Museum of Art. Object 255057. Also catalogued as Head of a youth. Roman, ca. 27 BCE–68 CE. CC0 1.0. Object page. Used for Apollo, Proof.

Hades — Bust of Serapis

No public-domain photograph of an ancient sculpture titled simply “Hades” was available at sufficient quality. This is the Serapis type, the Greco-Egyptian syncretism associated with Hades. Vatican Museums, Museo Pio-Clementino, inv. 689. Photograph by Jastrow (2003), public domain. Wikimedia file. Used for Hades, lessons and checkpoints.

Iris — West pediment of the Parthenon

The British Museum. ca. 447–433 BCE. The figure is headless, with the arms broken, and the portrait keeps that fragment. Photograph by Jastrow (2006), public domain. Wikimedia file. Used for Iris, Senses.

Hermes — Marble statue of Hermes

The Metropolitan Museum of Art. Object 254925. Roman, 1st or 2nd century CE. Pentelic marble. CC0 1.0. Object page. Used for Hermes, paths and dispatch.

Athena — Marble head and torso of Athena

The Metropolitan Museum of Art. Object 251476. Roman, 1st–2nd century CE. CC0 1.0. Object page. Used for Athena, Judgment.

Themis — Statue from Rhamnous

Pentelic marble, c. 300 BCE. National Archaeological Museum of Greece, Athens, NAMA 231. Photograph by Gary Todd, CC0 1.0. Wikimedia file. One forearm of the sculpture is missing. Used for Themis.

Nike — Terracotta statuette of a Nike flying

The Metropolitan Museum of Art. Object 247495. Greek, Myrina, early 3rd century BCE. CC0 1.0. Object page. Used for Nike.

Atlas — Farnese Atlas

Museo Archeologico Nazionale di Napoli. Roman, 2nd century CE, after a Hellenistic original. Photograph by Van Der Meulen Christofle, CC0 1.0. Wikimedia file. Visual patron only. No new capability claim.

Metis, Momus, Hephaestus, Titanos

AI-generated for this site in the same series. No public-domain sculpture photograph was used as the edit reference. Titanos is a figurehead for the engine, series-matched to the Zeus and Atlas portraits, not a claim about a particular ancient statue.

Aphrodite, Psyche, Eat, the Moirai, Calliope, Ligeia, Echo

AI-generated for this site. No public-domain sculpture photograph was used. Aphrodite holds preferences and does not write residual facts. Psyche is a state container, not a Kin and not evidence of awareness. Eat stays Eat. Clotho, Lachesis, Atropos, and Harmonia sit under Hermes. Calliope, Ligeia, and Echo are legions on Aphrodite.
